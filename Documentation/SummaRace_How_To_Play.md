# SummaRace — Complete Guide (Player + Teacher)

*Version: branch `experiment/endless-override-2`, 2026-08-23. Written for three readers: the
researcher/teacher running the study, a panelist who wants to understand the app, and any
developer who picks this up later. Every behaviour below was verified in the running game.*

---

## PART 0 — What this game is, in one paragraph

SummaRace teaches Grade-4 learners to summarize stories using **SWBST** — **S**omebody,
**W**anted, **B**ut, **S**o, **T**hen. A child reads a short story, races to collect its five
SWBST parts, puts them in order, then writes one summary sentence. There are **30 stories**:
10 sessions ("missions") × 3 difficulties (Easy, Average, Hard). The app is **100% offline**,
**never punishes** the child (no game over, no fail, no retry), and quietly records what each
child did so the research team can analyse it later.

---

# PART 1 — THE LEARNER'S JOURNEY (start to finish)

## 1.1 Opening the app
The **SummaRace** logo appears with a loading bar and a SWBST tip. This takes about 2 seconds.

## 1.2 First time on a tablet only — "What's your name?"
- The child types their name and picks a **runner** — the boy or the girl. Ms. Lumi greets
  them from the side of the screen.
- **An empty name is fine** — the game calls them "Runner". Nothing is ever refused here.
- Tap **LET'S GO!** This screen is shown **once per tablet**. The chosen runner is the
  character they race as, and a matching marker appears beside their name on the main screen
  and in the teacher's learner list.
- The choice is saved on their profile, so it can be changed later — a child is never stuck
  with a first-day decision for all ten sessions.
- *Note: the child's name is NOT the research ID. See Part 2.4 (participant codes).*

## 1.3 Main menu
- **TAP TO START** → the mission map.
- "Playing as [name]" with their badge is shown at the bottom.
- A small **Teacher** button sits in the bottom-right corner (Part 2).

## 1.4 Pick a Mission (the map)
- Ten tiles on a board, numbered 1–10 = the ten study sessions.
- **Open** missions are bright cards and visibly larger; **locked** ones are smaller, dimmed,
  and carry a quiet padlock.
- A small **"2/3"** badge on each tile = how many of that mission's three stories are finished.
  (Stars are never used here — stars mean *how well you did* on the story cards, and one icon
  must not carry two meanings.)
- Tapping a locked stop gives a friendly nudge: *"Your teacher opens the next mission!"*
- **Only the teacher can open the next mission** (Part 2.3). This is a research requirement,
  not a limitation of the child.

## 1.5 Pick a Story
Three cards for that mission:
| Card | When it opens |
|---|---|
| **EASY** | always open |
| **AVERAGE** | after Easy is finished |
| **HARD** | after Average is finished |

Each card shows the story's picture, its title, and **three stars = the race score** from the
last time it was played. The card being asked for wears a gold ring and a **PLAY** badge;
finished stories show **REPLAY**. Locked cards show a large centred padlock and a hint.

## 1.6 Stage 1 — READ (the story)
- The story is shown **one page at a time, five pages total**.
- **A voice reads each page aloud** automatically. The **VOICE: ON/OFF** button turns this off;
  **HEAR AGAIN** repeats the page.
- Tap **QUESTION!** to answer a question about that page. Three options: **A**, **B**, **C**.
  - **Correct** → the option turns green, Ms. Lumi cheers, warm praise appears.
  - **Wrong** → the correct answer is shown and highlighted. *Nothing is blocked;* the child
    always continues. Only the **first** answer is recorded.
- Tap **NEXT PAGE** to continue. On page 5 the button reads **START RACE!**
- **Leaving:** a BACK chip appears only *before the first answer* — after that the run has
  become study data, and the game gently says "Keep reading — you're nearly there!"

## 1.7 Stage 2 — RACE (collect the story parts)
**The briefing** names the story, shows the five SWBST chips, and Ms. Lumi says "Ready,
runner?". Tap **START!** → a 3-2-1-GO countdown on a cinematic camera.

**During the race:**
- The runner runs by himself. **The child only chooses answers.**
- At the top: a **tracker** with five slots (S · W · B · S · T) that fills as parts are collected,
  the current **question**, and **three answer cards**.
- **Two ways to answer:** tap the answer at the top, **or** tap the left / middle / right side
  of the screen to move into that lane.
- "Next part in Ns" counts down to the next question. Reading time is generous (about 12–17
  seconds per part) and Easy stories get the most.
- **Correct** → sparkle, a speed boost, the word flies into its tracker slot.
- **Wrong** → a gentle slow-down, the **patrol** runs up behind for a few seconds, and **the
  correct answer is shown**. The run continues.
- **The patrol NEVER catches the child.** It is a mood beat, not a threat. There is no game
  over and no restart, ever.
- **Pause** chip (top-right) → resume, or leave (two taps).
- At the finish: fireworks, a card showing all five correct parts, then → Arrange.

## 1.8 Stage 3 — ARRANGE (put the parts in order)
- Five labelled slots: **SOMEBODY, WANTED, BUT, SO, THEN**, and five story parts below.
- **Tap a part, then tap the slot it belongs in.** While a part is held, every empty slot
  brightens. **UNDO** takes back the last placement.
- Tap **CHECK ORDER**: correct parts lock in green; wrong ones return to the pool with a
  friendly wiggle.
- **Help appears automatically** — after a few tries a hint names the part being missed, and
  **after four attempts the game fills in the rest itself** and moves on. A child can never be
  stuck here.
- **Leaving:** the phone's BACK button asks *"Go back to the stories?"* (NO, STAY / YES,
  LEAVE). Leaving saves the run so far, marked as incomplete.

## 1.9 Stage 4 — WRITE THE SUMMARY
- The five SWBST parts are shown as a reminder list.
- The child types **one sentence** in their own words. A faint example sentence shows the shape
  ("Owen wanted a companion to comfort him, but…") — **it is not filled in for them**; writing
  it is the whole point.
- Tapping **SUBMIT** may bring a gentle nudge ("try to use one sentence", "say who it is
  about") **at most twice** — then the sentence is accepted whatever it says. Nothing is graded
  inside the app; the researcher's paper rubric is the real measure.
- **Leaving:** same as Arrange — BACK asks *"Go back to the stories?"* and an incomplete run
  is saved rather than lost.

## 1.10 Results
- **Mission Cleared!** with **1–3 stars** earned from the race (5 correct first-picks = 3 stars,
  3–4 = 2 stars, fewer = 1 star).
- Praise, the story's **Main Idea**, the child's own sentence, the race time, and five treasure
  gems showing which SWBST parts were right the first time.
- **NEXT STORY** → back to the story cards. When all three stories of a mission are done →
  back to the map with a celebration.

## 1.11 Leaving a screen, and leaving the app
The Android BACK button never closes the app by accident. Instead it answers per screen:
on menus it goes back one screen (with a NO/YES confirmation where it matters); in the Race
(via the pause chip), in Arrange and in Summary it offers *"Go back to the stories?"* —
leaving saves the run so far, marked as incomplete; in the Reader it works only before the
first answer (after that, a friendly "keep going" message). To exit the app entirely,
children use the phone's Home button.

# PART 2 — THE TEACHER / RESEARCHER MODULE

Everything here is behind a PIN. Reach it from **Main menu → Teacher** (bottom-right corner).

## 2.1 First time on a tablet — setting the PIN
1. Tap **Teacher**.
2. The screen says: *"Teacher setup — Hold OK for 3 seconds to set this tablet's PIN."*
   **Hold** the button. (A hold, not a tap, so a curious child cannot claim the tablet — the
   first person to reach this screen owns the PIN, and undoing that costs a full erase.)
3. Type a PIN (4+ characters) → **NEXT** → type it again → **NEXT**.
4. **Do this on every tablet during installation, before any child uses it.**

⚠️ The PIN is stored only as a scrambled fingerprint — it cannot be read back off the device,
by anyone. There is no master PIN (a built-in one would be readable inside the app file).

## 2.2 Every time after — entering
Tap **Teacher** → type the PIN → **OK**. Five wrong tries = a 30-second wait.

**If the PIN is lost:** on the PIN screen, leave the box **empty** and **hold OK for 6 seconds**
→ "Reset this tablet?" → tap **ERASE TABLET** twice. ⚠️ **This erases every learner, all
progress, and all unexported data on that tablet.** Export first (2.5). It exists so a lost PIN
is recoverable, not as a routine tool.

## 2.3 Unlock next session
**Unlock next session** opens the next mission on the map for the learner using this tablet.
Do this at the start of each of the ten session days.

*Why the child can't do it:* the study requires every learner to get the same amount of
treatment. If a fast child could open mission 5 on day 2, the comparison with the control group
would measure practice time instead of skill.

## 2.4 Participant code (the link to the paper tests)
**Participant code** stores the child's ID from their paper booklet (e.g. `P07`) on this
tablet. 2–12 letters/numbers; duplicates are refused.

**This is the most important teacher task after the PIN.** The child's typed name is not
reliable (spelling, nicknames, two children with the same first name). Without the code, that
child's app data cannot be matched to their pretest/posttest score. The code is stamped on
every row of data.

## 2.5 Export logs
**Export logs** writes one combined file of everything on that tablet, and shows the exact file
path on screen. It also writes a companion list of learners.

- **Export after every session**, not once at the end. A broken or wiped tablet loses
  everything since its last export.
- The file lives in the app's own folder on the device — copy it off over USB. **Test this path
  on a real tablet before day 1**, since Android sometimes hides that folder.
- If there is nothing to export, the app says so plainly (it does not pretend to succeed).

## 2.6 Switch learner / + New learner
**Switch learner** lists everyone on the tablet (badge · code · name · session). Choosing "+ New
learner" asks for the **participant code first**, then sends the child to the name screen.
Use this if a tablet is shared between children.

## 2.7 Music
Turns background music on/off for the whole app. Narration is unaffected — narration is an
accessibility support the study relies on, and stays on unless a child turns VOICE off.

## 2.8 Delete all data
Two taps, and it erases every learner and every log on that tablet. **Use only after the study
data has been exported and safely stored** — it is the promise made to parents on the consent
form. The teacher PIN survives so a wiped tablet is never handed back unlocked.

# PART 3 — WHAT THE APP RECORDS (plain English)

One row per story played, stored on the tablet, never sent anywhere. It includes: which story;
the child's participant code; **their first answer to each reading question**; **their first
choice at each of the five race parts** (and which wrong option they chose); how many tries the
ordering took and whether the game helped; the sentence they wrote; stars earned; and how long
each stage took. Runs that are interrupted are saved too, marked as incomplete.

Two things by design: the patrol never catches anyone, so "times caught" is always 0; and only
**first** answers count, because a second attempt is practice, not a measure.

---

# PART 4 — INSTALLATION CHECKLIST (per tablet, before day 1)

1. Install the APK (all tablets from the **same computer** — otherwise updates won't install
   over the old app without erasing data).
2. Open the app once.
3. **Teacher → hold OK 3s → set the PIN.**
4. **Teacher → Participant code →** enter this child's booklet ID.
5. Hand it to the child; they type their name and pick a badge.
6. Confirm mission 1 is open on the map.
7. Test **Export logs** on at least one tablet and confirm you can copy the file over USB.

**Each session day:** Teacher → Unlock next session → child plays the three stories → Teacher →
Export logs.

# PART 5 — QUICK ANSWERS

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can a child fail? | No. No game over, no restart, no wrong-answer penalty beyond a brief slow-down. |
| Can a child skip ahead? | No. Sessions are teacher-unlocked; difficulties unlock in order. |
| Is there a timer? | No countdown clock. The race shows when the next part is coming, and Results shows the race time afterwards. |
| Is there internet / ads / purchases? | None. The app is fully offline by design. |
| Does the patrol catch you? | Never. It appears briefly after a wrong answer and falls behind. |
| Where is the exit button? | BACK works on every screen: on menus it goes back; mid-story it asks "Go back to the stories?" and saves the run as incomplete. Only the Reader (after the first answer) says "keep going" instead. Home exits the app. |
| What if the child taps the wrong SWBST part? | The correct answer is shown, the run continues, and the miss is recorded. |
| Can the app grade the summary? | No, and deliberately so — the paper rubric is the measure. The app only nudges gently, twice at most. |
| Who can see the child's data? | Only whoever has the tablet's PIN. Nothing leaves the device. |
| Can a child choose their character? | Yes — boy or girl, on the first screen, changeable later. It affects only who is on screen, never the task or the data. |
| Where are the correct answers? | `SummaRace_Answer_Key.md` — all 30 stories, their SWBST parts and every reading answer, generated from the game's own data. |
| How is the game organized? | `SummaRace_Game_Organization.md` — missions, stories, stages, difficulty, what is recorded, and the rules the game will not break. |
