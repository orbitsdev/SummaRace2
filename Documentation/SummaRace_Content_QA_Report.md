Audit date **2026-08-05**, branch `experiment/endless-override-2`.
Scope: every string in `Assets/_Game/Resources/Stories/*.json` measured against the geometry
it actually renders into, plus a corpus-level validity audit of the SWBST content.

Nothing here was taken on trust. Every constant was read out of the source that owns it
(`EndlessRaceDirector.cs`, `MainSummaRace.unity`, `Reader.unity`, `Arrange.unity`,
`Summary.unity`, the TMP font assets and their source TTFs), and every claim below is a
number this pass computed.

## 2. Task 1 — does every string actually render?

`fit.py` measures each string in the rect it lands in, in its real font, and solves for the
size TMP's auto-sizer would choose. Geometry read, not assumed:

| Surface | Rect | Font | Auto-size | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Race answer card | **1.275 × 0.730 world units** | Fredoka-SemiBold | 0.2 – 2.4 | `PlaceAnswerGate` + `BuildCard`; `cardWidth = min(1.55, laneOffset*0.95)` = **1.425**, `laneOffset: 1.5` in `MainSummaRace.unity` |
| Collect-to-slot token | 564 × 152 px | Fredoka-SemiBold | 26 – 58 | `FlyCollectedToSlot` |
| SWBST tracker slot | 118 × 80 px, **NoWrap + Ellipsis** | Fredoka-SemiBold | 24 – 50 | `BuildTracker` |
| Reader option pill | **765.3 × 173.4 px** | Nunito-Regular | 26 – 44 | `Reader.unity` anchors, margin (30,4,22,4), + `<indent=9%>` |
| Reader question | 816.4 × 228.5 px | Nunito-Bold | 34 – 52 | `Reader.unity` |
| Reader page text | 807.4 × 822.9 px | Nunito-Regular | 40 – 66 | `Reader.unity` |
| Arrange piece / slot label | 912.4 × 119 / 912.4 × 124.8 px | Nunito-Regular | **fixed 32 / 34, overflow=Overflow** | `Arrange.unity` |
| Summary reference block | 914.1 × 388.6 px | Nunito-Regular | **fixed 32, overflow=Overflow** | `Summary.unity` |

**Two things had to be right or the whole measurement lies, and both were traps:**

1. **The TMP font assets are `m_AtlasPopulationMode: 1` (Dynamic).** Their baked
   `m_CharacterTable` holds only glyphs some scene has already used — **69 characters for
   Fredoka, 44 for Nunito**. Fredoka's baked table has no `J`, `Z`, `j`, `z`, `-`, `:` or
   `(`, all of which occur in the stories. Advances therefore come from the **source TTFs**
   (`Fredoka-SemiBold.ttf`, `Nunito-Regular.ttf`, `Nunito-Bold.ttf`, parsed directly:
   `head`/`hhea`/`hmtx`/`cmap`). Cross-checked against Unity's own baked values: **maximum
   disagreement 0.0075 font units at pointSize 90**, i.e. 0.008% of an em.
2. **World-space TMP under a perspective camera carries a ×0.1 scale**
   (`orthographicMultiplier = m_isOrthographic ? 1 : 0.1f`). A race card's "2.4" is
   **0.24 world units per em**. Miss it and every race number is out by a factor of ten.

## 3. Task 2 — content validity

### 3.1 Structure — 30/30 pass

All 30 stories: `id` matches filename, `session`/`difficulty` match the filename, 5 pages,
5 elements in exact **S-W-B-S-T** order, 3 distinct options per question, `correctIndex` in

fields, `title`/`mainIdea`/`heroImage` non-empty. **All 30 hero images and all 150 narration
paths resolve to a real file on disk.**

### 3.2 Can a gate be passed without reading? (the race)

Every score is an **expected value** — a tie between *k* cards is credited 1/*k*, so nothing
is flattered by a best case. The null is a **permutation test** (4000 trials): hold the three
strings fixed, randomise which one is correct. That is the exact null for "this signal carries
no information", and it is not a flat 1/3 once ties exist.

| strategy | score | null | z |
|---|---|---|---|
| tap the card with the most characters | 50.8% | 33.3% | +4.9 |
| tap the card with the smallest print | 42.6% | 33.3% | +3.2 |
| tap the card with the most words | 31.7% | 33.3% | −0.5 |
| tap the card with the fewest lines | 28.8% | 33.3% | −2.9 |
| tap the card with the biggest print | 22.9% | 33.3% | −3.6 |
| tap the card with the fewest characters | 17.8% | 33.3% | −4.2 |

**Statistically detectable, and practically zero.** Two facts settle it:

1. **Every card is the same physical size.** `PlaceAnswerGate` builds all three at
   `new Vector2(cardWidth, 0.85f)`, `cardWidth = 1.425` for every lane. The F44-era cue — a
   visibly wider card — *no longer physically exists*. The only thing a longer string can do
   now is render smaller text on an identically sized card.
2. **The effect size of that is sub-pixel.** Comparing `correct`'s rendered size against the
   smaller-printing distractor across all 150 sets: **median difference 0.000 font units**,
   mean −0.003; as on-screen cap height at 10 m, **median 0.00 px**, mean −0.04 px, p90
   1.64 px — against cards whose capitals are 18–29 px tall. In **63 of 150 sets (42%) all
   three cards render at literally the same font size**, and in 95 (63%) at the same line
   count. Character margins agree: mean **−0.1**, median **0**, and only 4 sets exceed
   `flag.py`'s ±6.

So the >33% score is the expected-value scorer splitting ties that a learner cannot see.
**No race content change is warranted, and churning it would risk F44's work for nothing.**

### 3.3 Answer-position balance

Reader `correctIndex` as stored in the JSON is heavily skewed — index 0: 48, **index 1: 96
(64%)**, index 2: 6 (4%). This needs **no content fix**:
`ReaderController.ShuffleDisplayOrder` Fisher–Yates shuffles the three buttons on every page
and maps the tap back through `_displayOrder`, so the stored index is not what a learner
sees. `EndlessRaceDirector.PlaceAnswerGate` does the same across the three lanes. Both were
read and confirmed. Position carries no information in either phase.

### 3.4 Race distractors reused verbatim from the Reader

50 of 300 race distractors (16.7%) are verbatim a Reader wrong option from the same page;
**0** come from a different page. Broken down by slot:

| slot | count |
|---|---|
| SOMEBODY | **47** |
| WANTED | 2 |
| SO | 1 |
| BUT, THEN | 0 |

47 of the 50 are `SOMEBODY` slots where the distractor is a **character name** — the cast of
a five-page story is finite, and "Beige Crayon", "The teacher", "Mzee" are the only wrong
answers that exist. That is not "passable from memory of the Reader"; it is the same three

*(This pass briefly made it worse: one lengthened Reader distractor came out exactly equal to

### 3.5 Slot appropriateness

The threat is **not** "the correct answer looks wrong for its slot"; it is "the correct
answer is the *only* one that looks right", which lets a learner who understands SWBST
eliminate two cards without understanding the story. Automated marker scan over the 90
`SOMEBODY` / `WANTED` / `BUT` sets: **1** case where `correct` was the only slot-shaped
option (`s04_hard` BUT), **15** where `correct` carried no obstacle marker while a distractor
did. All 16 were read by hand against their passage; **all 16 are false positives of the
keyword rule** — e.g. `s01_hard` BUT "Every animal he researched had disadvantages" is an
obstacle, the regex simply has no entry for *disadvantages*. The one genuinely awkward case
is noted in §4 (`s03_hard` BUT), and it is the researcher's own SWBST line, so it stays.

### 3.6 Factual correctness of every `correct` line

Method: the entire corpus is only **12,943 characters of page text**, so all 30 stories were
read in full and all **150** `correct` lines checked by hand against their own five pages,
supported by a lexical-grounding score (content-word overlap with the passage, mean 0.75 —
low by construction, because SWBST lines are summaries, not quotations).

**0 of 150 contradict their story.** The `s09_hard` class of error (SWBST claiming Marusia
*found* the flower when she *told Baba Yaga where it was*) does not recur; that slot now
reads "She told Baba Yaga where the magic flower grew", which matches page 4. Weakly grounded
but **not wrong** lines, listed for the researcher rather than as defects: `s04_easy` BUT,
`s04_average` WANTED, `s06_easy` BUT, `s10_hard` WANTED, `s10_hard` SO.

### 3.7 Spelling and typography

**Zero** pattern hits across every learner-visible string in all 30 stories (titles, main
ideas, page text, questions, all reader options, all element lines): no double spaces, no
space before punctuation, no repeated words, no missing space after a full stop, no lowercase
standalone `i`, no curly-quote mixing, and no split word of the `Baba Yag a` kind. 1,379
distinct word forms; 495 occur exactly once and were eyeballed — all are legitimate
low-frequency vocabulary or proper nouns.

## 5. Remaining defects, in priority order

### P1 — `SOMEBODY` is truncated in the race SWBST tracker — ✅ **CLOSED in `dca9b27`**

> **Resolved, and not by the fix proposed below.** Dropping `fontSizeMin` to 20pt would have made
> the framework's own first word the smallest text on the HUD. Instead the plaques were widened
> **138→160** (gap 12→10, board 774→880), so `SOMEBODY` fits at the full 24pt floor. Verified in
> play mode in `de63a3d`. The measurement below is kept because it is the evidence, and because it
> also flags `WANTED` and `THEN` as having been *exactly* on the 118px edge — a reason not to
> narrow the plaques again.

*Original finding, as measured on 2026-08-05:*

`EndlessRaceDirector.BuildTracker` gives each plaque a 138×96 rect with a (10,8) inset, i.e.
a **118×80** label, `TextWrappingModes.NoWrap`, `TextOverflowModes.Ellipsis`,
`fontSizeMin = 24`. `RefreshTracker` fills a collected slot with `type.ToUpperInvariant()`.

Measured in Fredoka-SemiBold: `SOMEBODY` needs **128.3 px at 24pt in a 118 px slot — 9%
over**, so the auto-sizer bottoms out at its floor and the plaque ellipsises. It needs
**22.1pt** to fit. `WANTED` and `THEN` land at exactly 118 px, i.e. on the edge. The label
also carries `FontStyles.Bold` over an already-semibold face, so TMP's synthetic bold widens
it further than this model accounts for — the real overflow is worse than 9%.

This is the **first** slot of every one of the 30 races, and it is the word that names the
framework the whole instrument teaches.

Fix (one line, in `BuildTracker`) — `lbl.fontSizeMin = 24f;` → `lbl.fontSizeMin = 20f;`.
This is outside this pass's remit (no `.cs` edits); it is not fixable from `overrides.json`
because the string is `SwbstPalette`-side, not content-side.

## 7. How to re-run

```sh
cd Tools/StoryPipeline
python apply.py     # overrides.json -> the 30 JSONs (content-idempotent)
python flag.py      # content gate:  must print "flagged: 0"
python fit.py       # geometry gate: must print 0 failures on every content surface
python qa.py        # corpus audit: strategy scores, margins, reuse, grounding, typos
python qa.py --json out.json   # same, machine-readable
```

`fit.py` and `qa.py` read all their constants from the code and scenes, so if anyone resizes
a race card, retunes `laneOffset`, or moves a Reader anchor, both pick it up on the next run
rather than going quietly stale. Their only inputs from this document are the two judgement
calls, both stated in the source: the playtested-floor definition (`fit.py`, derived from
`s01_*`) and `LEGIBLE_CAP_PX = 20` (used only to express reading time, never to pass or fail).
